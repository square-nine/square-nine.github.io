The last few days have been some of the most interesting. I've gotten sore throats before, and additionally been ill in other ways. Everyone gets ill at some point, it's how the immune system evolves. But, after a very stressful weekend navigating back to Southampton from London amidst Victoria and Northern line closures between Euston and Waterloo, it was enough to shut down my voice.

## What does it feel like?

It's... very unique. I remember being quite reserved and quiet during my high school days, but university became my chance to vocalise more. And first year still wasn't quite that opportunity as I was still figuring out what it's like living at university. So it's only been for the few months of Year 2 that I have been able to "speak up" more, if you will. Talking to people is great! But with my voice being shutdown, now I can't really talk.

> Try limit your speaking if you have a sore throat, better to keep quiet now than to be unable to speak later!
{: .prompt-warning }

## "Can't really talk"?

It's not quite as destructive as "I cannot speak". Instead, it's like the volume slider is literally dragged down on me to something like 10%. 

## How is it affecting you?

I've been able to enjoy myself. As my pre-uni days, I can find the joy in the silence and just work, play and live for one. So for the child within me, he's happy that I can just slip through conversations. But, here's the twist: I'm a very different man. I love to chat about some stuff, especially after one of my more hectic weeks. To be robbed of my speech means that I can't indulge myself in sharing some of the cool things that's happening. Also, this wasn't a sudden thing. It's been slowly getting worse as my sore throat began affecting my voice. Which really fustrated me during this event I was at for the weekend...

### MathsJam 2026

I've had the pleasure of going with my girlfriend to the Annual MathsJam Gathering UK 2026. This is 2 days of people speaking about maths as well as presenting various maths-related things. This included a mathsy bake-off where the cakes and treats are based on something maths-related. There was also the "Competition Competition", which is when people had a competition they would submit to the Competition Competition, and then others at the MathsJam could enter to the competitions. Then each competition announced a winner, and there was a winner for the best competition from those competitions! Also, all the food and accommodation were covered so it was just the cost of travel to get there and back. Which wasn't cheap, but compared to the subsidation I think it was worth it, financially. Little did I know how much it'd cost in other aspects...

I had the pleasure of being one of the speakers, with my presentation being about the feeling of doing maths at the university level. My voice wasn't nearly as bad as it is now, but it certainly wasn't the flowers and roses it was before I got ill. I was worried it wasn't clear but apparently it was. Anyways, 5 minutes of non-stop talking when my throat was sore definitely affected me.

To wrap up the first day, I played some board games (another regrettable choice with deteriorating vocal chords) and listened in to the "MathsJamJam", a selection of parodies of other songs with a mathematical twist. Ironically, there was a song called "Zeno". Which of course, everyone sung along as "Zee-no" instead of my name as "Zay-No" but that's fine: different lingustic sources.

And that's one of the major points that really got me annoyed about my voice. It was meant to be a sing-along to support the person who created the song as well as make it more lively. I really wanted to join in but my voice was very raspy by then so I couldn't. I do think I still enjoyed the day lots, but due to the illness it really just made it much less enjoyable compared to if I wasn't ill. But that's with most things. I really wanted this break, so I didn't choose to cancel it.

## How did my voice actually disapper?

After an equally as exciting second day at MathsJam, "jammed" with more talks, it was time to leave. To accompany me was Shub and L (anonymised for the fun of it), and he had some fun showing us some maths puzzles, like how by rearranging some cards with robots printed on them it'd be possible to create a "new" robot. That occupied us from Milton Keynes to London Euston.

Once we arrived, we were just heading down to the Victoria line as part of a chain to get to Waterloo when, all of a sudden, it appears a **FIRE** appeared on the exact line we needed to take back. Faced with potentially missing our train back, I had to think of where to navigate. L isn't used to the London system and Shub as well, so as the "native" Londoner I had to vocalise where to go as a navigation. Missing the simple fact that we could have backtracked on the Northern line to then get to the Bakerloo line, which would have been much simpler, in the heat of the moment I devised a slighly more evil route.

### Buses...

Herding through the crowd of both people departing the cancelled Jubliee, as well as the oblivious still making it down to the platform, I dragged them up to the bus station. There is a bus that goes directly to Waterloo. But what I forgot to account for is that there were a *lot* of people also making the Euston-Waterloo transfer. So I found out another bus also does the same route but departs from a bus stop a short walk away. Once making it there, it turns out that one was also crowded, but we got onto it anyways. 

Then what graced us was 15 minutes of crowded bus travel. Standing, jam packed between people to the point where people were standing next to the cabin, I considered just getting off for the fresh air. Don't forget I was coughing here too, and my voice was slowly vanishing as I had to corrall my compatriots. Finally, after what felt like forever, we managed to get off at Waterloo! But we missed our train, so that was an additional half hour of waiting. Fortunately L works near Waterloo, so he guided the final stretch. The stress of this meant that my voice was beginning to go, and after we split from L it was gone then.

## The great charades

To still communicate with Shub despite my voice going, it was time to play charades. Pointing and gesturing is what carried me most of the time, and I must say it's both fun and annoying. Indicating some things become super quick, and some things are impossible (and I need to type it out to be read!). Despite that, we managed to get food and navigate to the second train.

> Highly recommend not being ill.
{: .prompt-info }

### The 2nd Train...

This one, due to the delays preventing people from the first one, was also packed. WE couldn't find seats when boarding, so we had to post up by the door, standing with our bags by our feet and being cramped again. Once again, I'm still coughing and my voice is well and truly lost. It was also really tough to go through that as well, though fortunately many people weren't travelling far and we managed to find seats halfway into the journey. And then I felt rested, and was able to reflect a bit on the absurdity of this situation. I was so tired and yet there was lots to go.


## The Aftermath

Once we pulled up to Southampton (fortunately without delays like the last time we took the train) it was just a bus and a short walk back. I regret overextering myself, but in conclusion I kind of had to such that we could all be guided. It was still a great two days reprieve but having to limit your talking in one of the few places where talking is greatly encouraged really messed with me. I wished that I could have said hundreds of words more and yet I had to, was forced to, keep quiet. 

Being quiet is something which the little me could enjoy because I knew I had the freedom to speak. Nowdays it's a restriction when I've flipped the dynamic, and turned myself into a position where my words are usually required but I have the freedom to keep quiet. It'll take time to get better, but I've bought myself a bit of time through this.

> See you \*cough cough\* around!
